# MyZubster / Zorgax — Research Consent Protocol

## Core principle

Participation in the MyZubster / Zorgax pilot, use of the marketplace,
GitHub contribution, participation in LIFE-aligned technical work,
or other community activity does not constitute research consent.

Research participation is voluntary and requires separate explicit consent.

## Before consent

The prospective participant must be informed of:

- research purpose;
- activities requested;
- categories of data collected;
- intended analysis;
- retention policy;
- publication/anonymisation approach;
- voluntary nature of participation;
- withdrawal procedure;
- relevant researcher/institution contact information once formally defined.

## Consent states

NOT_REQUESTED
INVITED
INFORMATION_PROVIDED
CONSENT_PENDING
CONSENT_CONFIRMED
DECLINED
WITHDRAWN

Only CONSENT_CONFIRMED permits activation as a research participant.

## Data handling

Research records should use a pseudonymous research ID.

Public GitHub usernames should not automatically become the research
participant identifier.

Private contact details must not be stored in the public repository.

Wallet addresses are not required for research participation.

Consent must not be written on-chain.

## Evidence separation

PUBLIC CONTRIBUTION EVIDENCE
    ↓
RESEARCH CANDIDATE

does not imply:

RESEARCH PARTICIPANT

Activation requires:

EXPLICIT RESEARCH CONSENT
    +
PSEUDONYMOUS RESEARCH ID
    +
HUMAN APPROVAL

## Human gate

No automation may:

- infer research consent;
- automatically enrol a candidate;
- publish private participant information;
- contact candidates in bulk;
- claim university affiliation;
- claim EU LIFE/CINEA endorsement;
- infer institutional partnership.

Any transition to CONSENT_CONFIRMED must be reviewed by a human maintainer.

## Institutional review

Before using this protocol for formal academic research, the responsible
researcher/institution must determine the applicable ethics, informed-consent,
data-protection and institutional-review requirements.

This repository protocol is an engineering governance mechanism and is not,
by itself, institutional ethics approval.
